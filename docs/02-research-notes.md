## Interview questions — students (template)

Use these as a starting guide. Ask follow-up questions when an answer is interesting. Record what the person actually said, in their own words where possible.

1. Tell me about the last time you missed a lecture, or left one with incomplete notes. What happened?
2. What did you do to catch up? What worked, and what did not?
3. How do you decide whether a summary or a set of notes is trustworthy?
4. Have you ever learned something from a summary or a classmate's notes that turned out to be wrong? What happened?
5. If you had a summary with a source link for every line, would you use it? What would make you trust it — or not trust it?
6. What would you want practice questions to do for you — test memory, test understanding, or both?
7. What would stop you using a tool like this?

## Interview questions — lecturer (template)

The most important lecturer question is asked directly, because it defines a guardrail for the whole product:

> **What must never be changed or omitted in a summary of your lecture?**

Ask it exactly, and record the answer in full. Then ask:

1. What must never be changed or omitted in a summary of your lecture?
2. Which definitions in your course are most often confused or merged by students?
3. Are there points where a small change in wording changes the meaning? Give an example.
4. Would you review a summary of your own lecture before students see it? How much time would that realistically take?
5. What would make you approve a summary — and what would make you reject it?
6. What should happen when a handwritten note is hard to read? Should the tool guess, or flag it?
7. What are your concerns about copyright in slides, or about students relying on a summary instead of attending?

## Patterns to look for (after all 5 interviews)

Do not fill this in before the interviews. After them, look for:

- Do students check a summary against the original, or trust it as written?
- Is the biggest pain missing content, wrong content, or not knowing what is missing?
- Does the lecturer care most about definitions, numbers, examples, or sequence?
- Is "flag as uncertain" acceptable to students, or do they want a best guess?
