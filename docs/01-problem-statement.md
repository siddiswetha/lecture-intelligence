# 01 — Problem Statement

## The problem, in one sentence

Students miss lectures, take incomplete notes, and revise from fragmented sources — and an ungrounded summariser that invents definitions makes that worse, because students then learn wrong information as fact.

## What is happening today

- A student misses a lecture because of illness, work, travel, or a timetable clash. They borrow notes, but the notes are incomplete.
- A student attends but cannot write fast enough. Key definitions are half-copied. A worked example is missing its final step.
- At revision time, the student pieces together slides, a friend's notes, a chat message, and a search result. No single source is complete, and the pieces do not always agree.
- Faculty repeat the same clarifications every term, because the same misunderstandings keep appearing.

## Why this matters

A summary is not a convenience here. It is a learning source. If a summary invents a definition, changes a number, or merges two similar ideas into one, the student does not just get a poor summary — they learn something wrong and may carry that error into an exam or into later study.

That is why the product challenge is **accuracy and traceability, not summary length**. A short summary that is fully supported by the lecture is far more useful than a long, fluent summary that contains one invented definition.

## Who has this problem

| User | How the problem affects them |
|---|---|
| Students who miss a lecture | They have no reliable way to catch up. Borrowed notes are incomplete and hard to trust. |
| Students with incomplete notes | They cannot tell what they missed, or whether what they wrote down is correct. |
| Lecturers | They repeat the same clarifications every term and cannot check every summary students use. |
| Course teams | They have no way to know whether a study aid is accurate before students rely on it. |

## What success looks like

A student who missed a lecture can read the summary alone, answer 5 practice questions from it, and be confident that every answer is grounded in what the lecturer actually taught. A lecturer can review the summary, trace any line back to the source, and approve it — or correct it — before students see it.

## What this project will not do

- It will not add knowledge from outside the lecture, even if that knowledge is correct.
- It will not guess what an unclear handwritten word says.
- It will not reproduce long passages of copyrighted slide text.
- It will not replace attending the lecture. It is a catch-up and revision aid, not a substitute for teaching.

## The core product decision

**Fidelity first.** Every summary line must be traceable to a source line. If a point cannot be traced, it does not go in — or it is flagged as uncertain. This rule is more important than coverage, length, or fluency. The eval plan in `docs/04-eval.md` exists to enforce it.
